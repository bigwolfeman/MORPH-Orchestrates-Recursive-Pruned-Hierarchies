# Paper note: Variational Sequential Monte Carlo (VSMC)

- **Title:** Variational Sequential Monte Carlo (Christian A. Naesseth, Scott W. Linderman, Rajesh Ranganath, David M. Blei; AISTATS 2018)
- **arXiv or URL:** 1705.11140 (v2, 2018-02-21). The id and authors were read from the paper's own first page.
- **Local paper text:** docs/references/smc-latent-objectives/vsmc-1705-11140/ (paper.txt, paper.pdf, SOURCE.md)
- **Date first noted:** 2026-10-19

## 1. Why we reference it

- 2026-10-19: Wolfe's reading, 2026-10-19: VSMC "has multiple gaussian distributions to select from, which is probably what we want here". This note checks that reading against the text (see the proposal entry in section 2 and the correction in section 4).
- 2026-10-19: VSMC treats the whole SMC procedure as a variational family and the IWAE bound as its T = 1 case. It is the paper to read for what the K particles of our mixture particles are as a distribution over latents, and for the claim "set N proportional to T and the bound stays tight".

## 2. What the paper shows

- 2026-10-19 (Section 2, Eq. 1 to 3): ELBO `L(lambda) = E_q[log p(x,y) - log q(x; lambda)]`. SMC step: resample ancestors `a_{t-1}^i ~ Categorical(w_{t-1}/sum)`, propose `x_t^i ~ r(x_t | x_{t-1}^{a_{t-1}^i})`, append, reweight `w_t^i = f(x_t | x_{t-1}) g(y_t | x_t) / r(x_t^i | x_{t-1}^{a})`. The SMC posterior estimate is a weighted sum of Dirac masses `sum_i (w_t^i / sum w) delta_{x_1:t^i}` (Eq. 2). The evidence estimate is `p_hat(y_1:T) = prod_t (1/N) sum_i w_t^i` (Eq. 3).
- 2026-10-19 (Section 3, Algorithm 1, Eq. 4 to 6, Proposition 1, Theorem 1): the VSMC family. Run SMC with proposals `r(. ; lambda)` and then draw ONE trajectory with probability proportional to the final weight (`b_T`). The density of that draw, `q(x_1:T | y_1:T; lambda) = p(x_1:T, y_1:T) E[ p_hat(y_1:T)^-1 ]` (Eq. 5), has no closed form. The surrogate ELBO `L_tilde(lambda) = E[log p_hat(y_1:T)]` (Eq. 6) satisfies `log p(y) >= L(lambda) >= L_tilde(lambda)`. This is the same objective as FIVO and AESMC, derived as a lower bound on the ELBO of the VSMC family.
- 2026-10-19 (Section 3, Eq. 7 to 9): gradient split `grad L_tilde = g_rep + g_score`. `g_rep = E[grad log p_hat]` is the reparameterization part. `g_score` is the score-function part from the discrete ancestor choices (Eq. 8 gives a Rao-Blackwellized form with a control variate). "We found that ignoring the score function term ... leads to faster convergence and very little difference in final ELBO." The proposed gradient is `g_rep` alone (Eq. 9), a biased estimator that still maximizes an unbiased bound.
- 2026-10-19 (Section 5, Figure 3): scalar linear Gaussian model (A = 0.5, Q = R = C = 1, T = 2, N = 2): the variance of `g_score` is "several orders of magnitude higher" than `g_rep` even with the control variates. Ignoring `g_score` perturbs the optimal lambda. Final ELBOs with and without it are "very similar" (dx 10 and 25, dy 1 to 25, T = 10) and the biased run converges faster. The number of orders of magnitude is not given in the text.
- 2026-10-19 (Section 4): N = 1 gives structured VI (no resampling, the distribution is the proposal). T = 1 gives "variational importance sampling" (VIS) and the surrogate ELBO "is exactly equal to the IWAE lower bound". The IWAE latent approximation is then the VIS draw: sample N, pick one by weight. Surrogate ELBO of VIS is always at least as tight as VB (N = 1). "This result does not carry over to VSMC": resampling can give a looser bound than VB or VIS, though it does not in practice.
- 2026-10-19 (Section 4, Figure 2): VIS with ONE Gaussian proposal `r(x; lambda) = N(mu, sigma^2)` and N = 10 approximates a bimodal posterior `p(x|y) ∝ N(x;0,1) N(y; x^2/2, e^{x/2})`; standard VB with a Gaussian catches only one mode. The paper's point: "even a simple proposal can lead to a very flexible posterior approximation".
- 2026-10-19 (Section 4, theory, citing Del Moral 2004 Thm 8.3.2 and Berard et al. 2014): `KL(q || p) <= c(lambda)/N`. As T grows with `N = bT`, `KL <= -E[log p_hat / p] -> sigma^2(lambda) / (2b)` with `0 < sigma^2 < infinity`. So N proportional to T makes the approximation arbitrarily accurate as T goes to infinity. "Neither standard VB nor IWAE (VIS) have this property." Caveat in the appendix: it does not hold if `dim(x_t)` grows instead of T.
- 2026-10-19 (Figure 1, Introduction, Appendix A.4, Figure 6): toy `x_t ~ N(0,1)`, `y_t ~ N(x_t^2, 1)`. "As the length of the sequence T increases, naive importance sampling effectively collapses to use only a single particle." In panel (b) only one particle has non-negligible weight at time T. In A.4, `y_t = 3`, N = 2T: for high T the optimal IWAE lambda is close to the VB optimum, while VSMC stays close to the VSMC optimum. Panel 1c (and Figure 6) are plots without a printed number table.
- 2026-10-19 (Section 5, Table 1): linear Gaussian SSM, T = 25, `Q = 0.1^2 I`, R = 1, dx = 10, dy = 1, true `log p(y) = -236.9`. ELBO: bootstrap particle filter -6701.4, SMC with the (locally) optimal proposal -253.4, VSMC -237.8. "VSMC outperforms them both ... only 0.9 nats lower than the true log-marginal likelihood". Number of particles and iterations for this row: not recorded.
- 2026-10-19 (Section 5, Table 2): multivariate stochastic volatility, 22 currencies, monthly returns 9/2007 to 8/2017, T = 119. Structured VI 6905.1. N = 4, 8, 16: IWAE 6911.2 / 6912.4 / 6913.3 and VSMC 6921.6 / 6935.8 / 6936.6 (assigned by column order in the extracted table, which agrees with the text "almost 0.2 nats per time-step": (6936.6 - 6913.3)/119 = 0.196). Figure 4: with theta and lambda fixed from N = 16, raising N at test time (to 500 on the axis) gives IWAE "limited" gain and VSMC "significant" gain.
- 2026-10-19 (Section 5, Figure 5): deep Markov model of 105 macaque motor cortex neurons, T = 21, 700 train / 84 test trials, dx in {3, 5, 10}, N = 8. VSMC "gets to the same ELBO faster" than IWAE. The paper does not claim a better final ELBO here.
- 2026-10-19 (the proposal, as defined and as used): the definition is `r(x_t | x_{t-1}; lambda)`, a distribution over the next state conditioned on the particle's own ancestor state, applied to each of N particles with shared parameters lambda (Algorithm 1, step 8). In experiments it is always Gaussian: LGSSM `N(mu_t + diag(beta_t) A x_{t-1}, diag(sigma_t^2))`; stochastic volatility `r ∝ f(x_t | x_{t-1}) N(x_t; mu_t, Sigma_t)` (the prior tilted by a learned Gaussian); deep Markov model `r ∝ N(mu^x_lambda(x_{t-1}), exp(sigma^x_lambda(x_{t-1}))) N(mu^y_lambda(y_t), exp(sigma^y_lambda(y_t)))`, a product of a state-conditioned Gaussian and an observation-conditioned Gaussian, neural nets in lambda. The first two use free per-time-step parameters (`mu_t`, `Sigma_t`, ...) fitted to one dataset, not amortized across datasets. No experiment conditions on future observations `y_{t+1:T}`. The text of the definition says data "enter via the weights and (optionally) the proposal" (below Eq. 4).
- 2026-10-19 (variance collapse): the paper has no experiment or statement on a learned proposal variance collapsing. The only guard is the importance ratio in the weight. See section 3.

## 3. Our speculation

- 2026-10-19 Speculation: the importance weight `w = f g / r` contains `-log r`. Shrinking the proposal variance raises `r` at its own samples and so lowers `w`, unless `f g` rises as much. The objective therefore pays for a narrow proposal that the posterior does not support. Our earlier learned-noise arms (memory note, 2026-09-23, sigma to about 3e-4) had no `p/q` ratio: the noise was part of the model with no matching density term. This objective would not fix that unless `sigma` is made a proposal parameter with the ratio `p(eps)/q(eps)` in the weight. If instead `sigma` stays a model parameter and `eps` is the proposal with `p = q` (our current setup), the ratio is 1 and nothing in the bound prevents `sigma -> 0`: the bound is `-log mean_k p(row | eps_k)` and noise can only hurt `p(row | .)`.
- 2026-10-19 Speculation: for our setup, the mixture of Gaussians Wolfe wants is available in one real form. After resampling, a new particle's proposal is a mixture `sum_i w_bar^i r(x_t | x_{t-1}^i)` over ancestors (Algorithm 1 step 7 then 8; this mixture is my derivation, not a sentence in the paper). The "multiple gaussians" are per-ancestor Gaussians with different means. They differ because the ancestors differ, not because particles carry separate learned heads. In our loop that means the proposal for a particle at chunk t must depend on that particle's own carry state at t-1.
- 2026-10-19 Speculation: the proposal conditions on `y_t` only. For an LM the matching object is a proposal for `eps` at chunk t given tokens up to the end of chunk t (the observation being scored). Tokens past the chunk are never allowed.

## 4. Corrections

- 2026-10-19: Correction to Wolfe's reading ("multiple gaussian distributions to select from"). Right: the proposal is a Gaussian per particle, conditioned on that particle's ancestor (and, in the deep Markov model, on the current observation), and the parameters are shared. Wrong or incomplete: (1) the object VSMC defines is not a mixture of Gaussians. The particle set is a weighted set of Dirac masses (Eq. 2), and the returned sample is one trajectory chosen by final weight. The marginal density q (Eq. 5) is intractable and the paper never writes it as a Gaussian mixture. (2) The flexible, multimodal shape in Figure 2 comes from importance weighting a single Gaussian N = 10 times, not from several different learned Gaussians. (3) Particles do not each learn their own distribution. They all apply the same `r(.; lambda)` to different ancestor states. The mixture-of-per-ancestor-Gaussians picture holds only for the next-step proposal after resampling, and is derived above, not stated in the paper.
