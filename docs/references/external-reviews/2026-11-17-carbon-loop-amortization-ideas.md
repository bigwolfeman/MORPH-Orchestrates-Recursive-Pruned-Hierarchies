1. Why a state-per-span loop earns so little while a token loop earns
Next-token CE on web text
├── Per-token local composition and 49k-vocab sharpening (~3.0+ nats)
│   └── Needs intra-span prefix x_{s+1, 1..j}  --> Token loop sees it; slot loop does NOT
└── Cross-span information (~0.40 nats total at 5k)
    ├── Exact token/bigram copy (~0.26 to 0.34 nats, 65% to 86% of gap)
    │   └── Pooled vector loses token IDs; output pointer head takes it in 1 hop (bypasses loop)
    └── Non-copy semantic state (~0.12 to 0.19 nats total)
        └── Unconditional summary before seeing the query finishes in 1 pass
The mechanism from first principles
Next-token cross-entropy asks for $-\log p(x_{t+1} \mid x_{\le t})$ at every token $t$. That objective splits into two physically different jobs [my speculation].
First, the cross-span bit budget on web text is tiny once copying leaves the hidden state. Cutting every path across span boundaries costs 0.40 nats per token at 5k steps [measured in the repo: docs/lessons-learned-loops-and-spans.md]. Far-bigram and exact-copy tokens account for 65% to 85.7% of the strict slot model's gap to plain [measured in the repo: docs/lessons-learned-loops-and-spans.md]. A pooled vector cannot hold discrete token identities, so the output pointer head carries 0.31 to 0.405 nats directly from past tokens without touching the loop [measured in the repo: docs/span-only-model.md]. That leaves 0.12 to 0.20 nats per token of non-copy semantic context for the entire slot channel [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-21-span-reach-split.md].
Second, a slot state runs blind to the intra-span query [my speculation]. Slot $s$ finishes all $T$ loop passes at the end of span $s$, before any token of span $s+1$ enters [measured in the repo: CLAUDE.md]. Token $j$ inside span $s+1$ has already observed tokens $1 \dots j$ of span $s+1$, so a token loop can run query-specific retrieval and multi-feature composition conditioned on the exact words right before the prediction [my speculation]. Slot $s$ must instead compress history into a query-agnostic vector before knowing what span $s+1$ will ask about [my speculation]. Under cross-entropy over a high-entropy future span, the optimal unconditional state is a smooth marginal summary, and deterministic or sampled next-span heads collapse toward that marginal [measured in the repo: docs/lessons-learned-loops-and-spans.md]. Gathering a query-agnostic summary over earlier spans takes one attention pass: zeroing the series span stack costs 0.195 to 0.227 nats, and almost the whole value is already there at pass 1 [measured in the repo: docs/span-only-model.md]. Once pass 1 settles near its fixed point, the recurrence gradient equals a constant write gradient, leaving the slot core blocks quiet at 0.005 to 0.018 above the 0.865 DiagonalInjection floor [measured in the repo: lab/theory/tul_exploration/READ-BEFORE-TUNING-THE-LOOP-MAP-0.87-IS-THE-INJECTION-FLOOR.md].
Third, a token loop earns by multiplying effective parameters on each token's own logit map, and a coarse loop is starved of both mutable space and gradient samples. Looping $N_{\text{rec}}$ core parameters $T$ times multiplies effective parameters by $g(T,B)$, reaching 1.91 at 2 loops, 2.50 at 4, and 2.80 at 8 [from a paper: arXiv 2610.26030]. In a plain token loop, every token gets that extra nonlinear capacity on its own hidden state [my speculation]. In the boundary-depth probe, giving depth 6 only to span-boundary tokens kept 89.8% of those boundary tokens' own gain and gave the next 4 tokens 0.4% [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-14-plain-loop-boundary-depth-probe.md]. In the H-Net token loop, gain is lowest right after a sentence boundary [measured in the repo: /mnt/BigAssDrive/00projects/00DeepNet/hnet/ignored/worktrees/graph-loop/.agents/postmortem/2026-11-17-loop-in-hierarchy-side-work.md]. And on slot-spandec-strict-coretok, the exact same 6 core blocks earned K1-K6 +0.0102 on span-restricted tokens and +0.0005 on slot states [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-13-arc-loop-reads-tokens.md]. Meanwhile, a compressed loop with $s$ slots is bounded in $\text{DSPACE}(O(s d p + \log n + \log T))$ so extra passes add time rather than mutable memory [from a paper: arXiv 2605.30757], 99.6% to 99.8% of the MORPH slot carrier collapses to one shared vector after pass 1 [measured in the repo: docs/lessons-learned-loops-and-spans.md], carried content decays by about a third per pass [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-18-hop-distance-earning.md], and the coarse core sees 21x fewer position updates per step than a token layer,35M positions vs 738M in H-Net [measured in the repo: /mnt/BigAssDrive/00projects/00DeepNet/hnet/ignored/worktrees/graph-loop/.agents/postmortem/2026-11-17-loop-in-hierarchy-side-work.md].
Measurements that push back against this account
Two measurements show a coarse loop is not mathematically dead [my speculation]:
- On the 160k-step series span run, K1-K6 grew 3.7x from +0.0030 at 5k to +0.0110 at 160k, rising fastest during LR cooldown [measured in the repo: docs/span-only-model.md]. However, its parameter-matched 6-layer flat twin tied it on CE at 160k while training 1.38x faster [measured in the repo: docs/span-only-model.md].
- On prev-reach1, planted rare tokens $g$ spans back read exact 0.000 before pass $g-1$ and arrived on schedule at pass $g-1$, proving the slot core executes multi-hop gather when no 1-hop path exists [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-18-hop-distance-earning.md].
Repo laws and claims that are wrong or over-read
1. "LXTUL's fan and latent selection made the slot loop earn real depth (+0.0215 on MORPH, +0.0585 on Parcae)" is wrong on Parcae and suspect on MORPH [my speculation]. On Parcae, minimal_fan read K1-K6 0.0000, minimal_fan with latent selection read 0.0000, minimal_fan with the three stabilisers read 0.0000, and minimal_fan + epivol alone produced K1-K6 +0.0462 by making CE@1 0.057 worse and ending 0.011 worse at CE@6 [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-14-parcae-epivol-vs-stabilisers-5k.md]. Because epivol pushes cells apart on passes 1 and 2 only, it acts as a pass-indexed handicap that later passes undo [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-14-parcae-epivol-vs-stabilisers-5k.md].
2. Reading plain Parcae's K1-K6 (+0.0377 to +0.185) as multi-pass reasoning is over-read [my speculation]. Adding an Anchor skip h0 + u around the token-level Parcae loop cut K1-K6 by 47x from +0.0377 to +0.0008 while improving CE@6 from 3.7256 to 3.7112 [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-15-parcae-chunk-loop-5k.md]. On MORPH, switching from noise entry to prelude entry cut plain K1-K6 from +0.185 to +0.033 [measured in the repo: docs/tul-loop-contribution-history.md]. Most of an unskipped plain K1-K6 is pass-1-to-6 representation scale building from a small or noisy entry; only the matched-step lead over a depth-1 twin (+0.0674 at 20k on MORPH, 0.0403 bpb on H-Net) is real value [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-13-arc-depth-ladder-ship.md].
3. Lesson 2.5, "token-loop depth gain is private so passes cannot share state across tokens", is over-read from an eval-only freeze [my speculation]. That probe froze neighbours in a model never trained to read shallow neighbour KV [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-14-plain-loop-boundary-depth-probe.md]. In LPT, when passes 2..R are trained to read pass-1 KV with gradients kept into pass 1, shared pass-1 KV beats full-loop LT by 0.38 to 1.09 PPL; detaching that gradient collapses PPL from 14.91 to 33.21 [from a paper: arXiv 2610.02383].
2 & 3. Seven concrete ideas, ranked
FLOP reference ruler: A plain 6-pass token loop with 4 prelude blocks, 6 core blocks at mean 6 passes, and 4 coda blocks runs 44 block-equivalents per token [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-13-arc-depth-ladder-ship.md]. Full BPTT scales forward plus backward linearly with block-equivalents at 3 units per pass [from a paper: arXiv 2610.26030]. A depth-1 twin runs 14 block-equivalents, or 0.32x FLOPs [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-13-arc-depth-ladder-ship.md]. A strict 1-in-21 slot loop runs 9.7 block-equivalents, or 0.22x FLOPs [my speculation]. One cost unit below is one 5k-step run at batch $6 \times 1024$ on one RTX 5090.
Rank 1. Pass-1 shared KV + counterfactual top-k token loop (leaves strict TUL)
- Mechanism: Run pass 1 of the 6-block core on 100% of tokens and cache pass-1 K and V with gradients attached, following LPT [from a paper: arXiv 2610.02383]. A linear router scores each token's pass-1 state $r_i = \sigma(w^\top h_i^{(1)})$ and gathers a fixed top $k = 20\%$ of tokens per row into passes $2 \dots T$, where active tokens run Q and FFN projections and attend to the shared pass-1 KV plus active-set self-KV [my speculation]. Emit $y_i = h_i^{(T)}$ on routed tokens and $h_i^{(1)}$ on unrouted tokens in forward, with straight-through term $(r_i - \text{sg}(r_i)) \cdot \text{sg}(h_i^{(T)} - h_i^{(1)})$ in backward [my speculation]. Main CE alone trains the router via $\langle \nabla_{y_i}\mathcal{L}, h_i^{(T)} - h_i^{(1)} \rangle$, pushing compute onto the 10% of tokens that hold 77% of loop gain and away from the third of tokens where looping hurts, with zero skip bypass on routed tokens and fixed shapes [my speculation].
- FLOPs vs plain 6-pass token loop: 0.43x to 0.45x at $k = 20\%$ (~19.5 block-equivalents/tok: 4 prelude + 6 pass-1 + $5 \times 6 \times 0.20$ routed passes with no KV recompute on the 80% + 4 coda), or 0.40x at $k = 12.5\%$ [my speculation].
- Collisions with past results: Collides with H-Net's selective-token-depth-probe, which lost 0.008 bpb at eval-only using state-movement heuristics, and plain-loop-boundary-depth-probe, which routed boundary tokens [measured in the repo: /mnt/BigAssDrive/00projects/00DeepNet/hnet/ignored/worktrees/graph-loop/.agents/postmortem/2026-11-17-loop-in-hierarchy-side-work.md]. Pass-1 entropy confuses irreducible randomness with composable hops, whereas the counterfactual straight-through gradient directly scores whether extra passes lower token $i$'s CE [my speculation]. Also avoids the Anchor-skip trap of parcae-chunk-loop-5k because routed tokens have no pass-1 skip in forward [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-15-parcae-chunk-loop-5k.md].
- Cheapest kill experiment (2 units): Run plain_pointer with shared pass-1 KV and top-20% routing at 5k steps alongside a uniform Poisson(2) shared-KV control at matched ~20 block-equivalents/tok [my speculation]. Kill it if paired CE@6 loses to the uniform depth-2 control or if K1-K6 recovers less than half of plain_pointer's +0.038 [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-14-plain-loop-boundary-depth-probe.md].
Rank 2. Narrow-subspace all-token core loop, d_core = d/2 + shared pass-1 KV (leaves strict TUL)
- Mechanism: Keep token resolution on 100% of positions and compress channel width instead of sequence length [my speculation]. After the full-width $d$ prelude, project each token $d \to d/2$ with no skip connection around the core, run the 6-block weight-tied core at width $d/2$ and FFN width $d_{\text{ff}}/2$ for $T \sim \text{Poisson}(6)$ passes using LPT shared pass-1 KV, and project $d/2 \to d$ into the full-width coda [my speculation]. Because linear FLOPs scale as $(d_{\text{core}}/d)^2 = 1/4$, each core pass costs a quarter of a full block while keeping $d/2 = 512$ channels per token, which is 10.5x TUL's state capacity per 21-token span [my speculation]. Every token retains intra-span query conditioning, full multi-hop token attention, 21x more gradient positions than TUL, and one dense Tensor-Core GEMM shape with zero gather/scatter launches [my speculation].
- FLOPs vs plain 6-pass token loop: 0.37x to 0.39x (~16.5 to 17.0 full-block equivalents/tok: 4 full prelude + $6 \times 6 \times 0.25$ core passes + 4 full coda) [my speculation].
- Collisions with past results: Collides with 2026-09-09-arc-density-panel.md, where pruning MLP blocks to 0.25 gave dependence without value, and with SMELT, where narrowing the whole model regressed past 2 loops [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-09-arc-density-panel.md] [from a paper: arXiv 2609.01343]. density-panel ran under absmean ternary before norm_match, which starved branch out/in to 0.058 and collapsed carrier rank to 4.4 [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-09-arc-density-panel.md], whereas a dense variance-scaled $d/2$ core keeps full branch gain while leaving prelude, coda, and embeddings at full width $d$ [my speculation]. Also avoids decode-loop-5k, which failed because it looped only 2 coda blocks after a dead slot bottleneck [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-14-parcae-decode-loop-5k.md].
- Cheapest kill experiment (2 units): Train a 5k d/2-core token loop with pointer head and its d/2-core depth-1 twin on the Parcae testbed [my speculation]. Kill it if CE@6 trails plain_pointer by more than 0.02 nats or if the loop fails to beat its d/2 depth-1 twin on both K1-K6 and the hop-chain eval [my speculation].
Rank 3. Iterative pointer composition in the slot loop, P^(t+1) = A_slot^(t) P^(t) (stays in strict TUL)
- Mechanism: Keep strict 1-in-21 TUL geometry and give the slot loop ownership of multi-hop token dereferencing inside the 0.36 to 0.41 nat pointer channel [my speculation]. At pass 0, each span $r$ initializes $H_p = 4$ sparse pointer distributions $P_r^{(0)}$ over the token positions inside span $r$ [my speculation]. At each core pass $t = 1 \dots T$, slot $r$ computes causal slot-to-slot attention $A_{r,u}^{(t)}$ over earlier slots $u \le r$ and updates its pointer table by convex Markov composition $P_r^{(t)} = (1 - \alpha_r^{(t)}) P_r^{(t-1)} + \alpha_r^{(t)} \sum_{u < r} A_{r,u}^{(t)} P_u^{(t-1)}$ [my speculation]. When token $i$ in span $s+1$ runs its output pointer head, it attends both to standard 1-hop token keys and to earlier spans' keys reading their $T$-hop dereferenced token distributions $P_r^{(T)}$ [my speculation]. Every past span becomes a pre-chased index entry that preserves exact 49k-vocab token IDs across $T$ hops without squeezing them through a 1024-d vector and without needing to see span $s+1$'s query in advance [my speculation].
- FLOPs vs plain 6-pass token loop: 0.22x (~9.8 block-equivalents/tok; six $64 \times 64 \times 1024$ pointer-matrix products add under 0.1% FLOPs per token) [my speculation].
- Collisions with past results: Collides with pointer_cell_key, which gave -0.0074 paired CE on Parcae and -0.0073 on MORPH, and with hop-distance-earning, where hidden-state carry lost a third per pass [measured in the repo: docs/tul-design-history.md] [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-18-hop-distance-earning.md]. pointer_cell_key only altered span $r$'s key while still copying span $r$'s local tokens (1 hop), whereas $P_r^{(T)}$ transports exact distant target token probabilities across $T$ spans with zero continuous washout and zero 1-hop restriction tax [my speculation].
- Cheapest kill experiment (2 units): Add iterative pointer composition to arm=minimal and its depth-1 twin for 5k steps on the mixed web + hop-chain stream now in 2026-11-17-parcae-hop-tasks-slot-loop.md [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-11-17-hops/docs/experiments/planned/2026-11-17-parcae-hop-tasks-slot-loop.md]. Kill it if hard-hop chain accuracy does not beat the depth-1 twin across seeds AND web-text K1-K6 stays under +0.010 [my speculation].
Rank 4. Frozen looped-teacher top-64 KL + input belief channel on a cheap loop, LIFT-TUL (strict TUL or depth-2 token loop)
- Mechanism: Train a small (e.g., 350M) 6-pass plain token loop once, freeze it, and use its top-64 next-token distribution in two places while pretraining the cheap student [my speculation]. First, replace half the main loss weight with forward KL on the teacher's top-64 probabilities plus tail bucket, which strips web-text phrasing noise and concentrates gradient on the continuations a 6-pass token loop actually resolved [from a paper: arXiv 2609.38149]. Second, fuse the teacher's soft top-64 span-boundary or previous-token belief into the student's input embedding with 50% dropout for the first 85% of steps, switching to the student's own detached pass-T output for the final 15% [from a paper: arXiv 2609.38149]. No hidden-state regression is used anywhere; supervision lives strictly in vocabulary probability space [from a paper: arXiv 2609.38149].
- FLOPs vs plain 6-pass token loop: 0.34x for a strict TUL student (0.23x student + 0.11x forward-only 3x-smaller teacher), or 0.56x for a 2-pass token-loop student [my speculation].
- Collisions with past results: Collides with the bypass law when teacher input is fed without dropout or adaptation, and builds directly on H-Net's teacher state target, where centred logit MSE hurt by +0.0098 bpb while top-64 KL beat CE by 0.0579 bpb (0.182 nats, 16x seed spread) [measured in the repo: /mnt/BigAssDrive/00projects/00DeepNet/hnet/ignored/worktrees/graph-loop/.agents/postmortem/2026-11-17-loop-in-hierarchy-side-work.md]. Also collides with LIFT's own ablation showing that without the teacher, self-feedback ties baseline (+0.01 PPL), whereas teacher + channel beats compute-matched baselines even charging teacher FLOPs [from a paper: arXiv 2609.38149].
- Cheapest kill experiment (2 units, using an existing plain_pointer 5k checkpoint as teacher): Train arm=minimal and its depth-1 twin for 5k steps under top-64 KL from the existing plain-pointer-norm-5k-s2 checkpoint [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-14-plain-loop-boundary-depth-probe.md] [my speculation]. Kill it as a loop lever if student@6 minus twin@1 is no larger than under plain CE, even if absolute CE drops [my speculation].
Rank 5. Delta-rule fast-weight program compiled by the slot loop (stays in strict TUL)
- Mechanism: Turn the slot state from static data into a linear program that span $s+1$'s coda executes on every intra-span token [my speculation]. Give the slot loop a cross-attention read over frozen prelude token KV, and maintain a fixed-rank ($R = 8$) fast-weight matrix $M_s^{(t)} = U_s^{(t)} {V_s^{(t)}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ per slot across passes $t = 1 \dots T$ [my speculation]. At each pass $t$, the slot extracts key-value rules from past tokens and applies a delta-rule update $M_s^{(t)} = M_s^{(t-1)} + \beta_s^{(t)}(v_s^{(t)} - M_s^{(t-1)} u_s^{(t)}){u_s^{(t)}}^\top$, re-projected to rank 8 so depth 1 and depth 6 expose the exact same tensor shape [my speculation]. Inside span $s+1$'s coda blocks, each token $x_i$ executes $x_i \leftarrow x_i + U_s^{(T)}({V_s^{(T)}}^\top x_i)$, allowing query-specific feature mapping inside span $s+1$ while compiling the adapter once per span [my speculation].
- FLOPs vs plain 6-pass token loop: 0.23x (~10.1 block-equivalents/tok: strict TUL 9.7 + S-by-N cross-attention + rank-8 matvec of $16 d$ FLOPs/tok in coda) [my speculation].
- Collisions with past results: Collides with 2026-09-10-arc-slot-reread.md (+0.0003 K1-K6) and trajectory-prefix (+0.0342 pad-cell artefact) [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-10-arc-slot-reread.md] [measured in the repo: docs/lessons-learned-loops-and-spans.md]. reread failed because it ran on an unrestricted coda and squeezed its read back into one vector, whereas here the coda is strict and receives a rank-8 operator; keeping rank fixed at $R=8$ at every pass eliminates the trajectory-prefix pad-width confound [my speculation].
- Cheapest kill experiment (2 units): Train arm=minimal modified with a rank-8 delta-rule fast-weight coda adapter and its depth-1 twin for 5k steps [my speculation]. Kill it if paired CE@6 does not beat arm=minimal (3.763) by at least 0.015 nats or if loop minus depth-1 twin is inside seed noise [measured in the repo: docs/tul-design-history.md] [my speculation].
Rank 6. Micro-chunk (g=4) series loop + 4-token local coda window + sparse top-64 DiscoLoop bridge (leaves sentence-span TUL)
- Mechanism: Replace 21-token variable sentence spans with fixed 4-token chunks (256 chunks per 1024-token row, 5.25x more loop positions per batch, zero dynamic shapes) in series topology with no Anchor skip [my speculation]. Fix block-memory-5k's local-syntax deficit by letting each coda chunk attend to its own 4 tokens plus the 4 raw tokens of chunk $c-1$ only, while all context older than 4 tokens must pass through earlier chunks' loop states [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-15-parcae-block-memory-5k.md] [my speculation]. Between core passes, apply a cheap top-64 sparse DiscoLoop bridge $h \leftarrow h + \alpha \, \text{RMSNorm}(\text{softmax}_{\text{top-64}}(W_{\text{head}} h / \tau) W_{\text{emb}})$ on chunk states so pass outputs realign with discrete token embeddings rather than drifting into continuous washout [from a paper: arXiv 2607.00341] [my speculation].
- FLOPs vs plain 6-pass token loop: 0.39x (~17.2 block-equivalents/tok: 8 prelude/coda + $36/4$ chunk core + 0.2 for 1/4-rate vocab projection; block-memory chunk-4 measured 168.5 ms vs 283.0 ms plain) [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-15-parcae-block-memory-5k.md] [my speculation].
- Collisions with past results: Collides with parcae-chunk-loop-5k (Anchor skip killed depth to +0.0030), parcae-block-memory-5k (0-token coda window left a 0.168-nat CE gap), MORPH reach1 (where a 21-token previous-span window ate half the slot worth), and DiscoLoop's 13B-token crossover on global NLL [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-15-parcae-chunk-loop-5k.md] [measured in the repo: docs/tul-design-history.md] [from a paper: arXiv 2607.00341]. A 4-token window covers only local subword n-grams (matching MORPH's conv width 4) without swallowing the medium-range cross-chunk budget [my speculation].
- Cheapest kill experiment (2 units): Run chunk-4 block-memory + 1-chunk coda window (with and without the top-64 bridge, vs depth-1 twin) at 5k steps [my speculation]. Kill it if the 1-chunk window leaves CE@6 more than 0.03 nats behind plain_pointer (3.726) or drops K1-K6 below block-memory's +0.0072 [measured in the repo: docs/references/looping-depth/parcae/lxtul-testbed-2026-10-16/filings/2026-10-15-parcae-block-memory-5k.md].
Rank 7. LIDAS middle-block growth into a Poisson(3) shared-KV token loop (leaves strict TUL)
- Mechanism: Combine MORPH's p3 depth-ladder finding (Poisson(3) max 6 tied Poisson(6) CE at 20k while cutting passes from 44 to 26) with LIDAS middle-layer growth and LPT pass-1 KV sharing [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-13-arc-depth-ladder-ship.md] [from a paper: arXiv 2602.16490] [from a paper: arXiv 2610.02383]. Start training with 4 prelude + 3 core (1 pass) + 4 coda blocks, duplicate the 3 middle core blocks in-place (copying weights and optimizer moments without LR reset) to 6 core blocks, and turn on Poisson(3) recurrence with shared pass-1 KV [from a paper: arXiv 2602.16490] [my speculation]. Depth-grown models keep improving early-exit accuracy to the final layer instead of stalling at layer 18 [from a paper: arXiv 2512.08819] and gain up to 2x on word-copy primitives when middle blocks are looped [from a paper: arXiv 2602.16490].
- FLOPs vs plain 6-pass token loop: ~0.42x whole-run training FLOPs (final p3 + shared KV runs at ~24.5 block-equivalents/tok = 0.56x, and LIDAS growth saves 22% to 26% of pretraining FLOPs before full depth) [from a paper: arXiv 2610.31421] [my speculation].
- Collisions with past results: Collides with 2026-09-13-arc-depth-ladder-ship.md, where un-clamped p3 lost wall-clock efficiency because tail passes ran 1-2 rows per batch [measured in the repo: lab/experiments/failures/2026-09-13-arc-depth-ladder-ship.md]. Fix that by drawing one Poisson depth per microbatch (or microbatch-grouped continuous depth batching) so every pass runs at 100% batch occupancy [my speculation].
- Cheapest kill experiment (2 units): Run a 5k-FLOP-matched comparison on Parcae: LIDAS-grown p3 (3,000 steps at 4+3+4 depth 1, then 3,200 steps at 4+6+4 Poisson(3) shared KV, equal total FLOPs to 5,000 steps of p3) against p3 from scratch and d1 [my speculation]. Kill it if the growth transition spikes or trails p3-from-scratch on CE@3 or K1-K6 [my speculation].
4. One closed idea we were wrong to close: compute-scheduled loop depth (T=1 early, mixed depth late)
Keep Coconut-style latent-replacement curricula, learned-sigma arms, spectral caps, per-pass staged targets, hierarchies, energy text loops, and epivol closed; every one of those failed for a structural mathematical reason [measured in the repo: docs/lessons-learned-loops-and-spans.md].
