## Question

When the coda cannot see tokens before position 576 (only the last 448 tokens plus
every slot), does the plan become worth nats — or is the prefix pathway itself
unable to deliver plan content to the CE?

## Hypothesis

The plan pays once it is the only carrier. The CW eval screen measured +0.029 nats
(CW1 vs CW3) for slots that were never trained under forced reliance; a co-trained,
70%-retrieval-accurate plan under a hard cut should be worth well more than the
0.01 gate. If it is still zero, the fault is the W_prefix/coda pathway or the plan's
content basis, not the planner.

## The unregistered discovery: positions pay, content does not

`val/plan_nats` (CE with the slots REMOVED minus normal CE) grew **0.27 → 0.81
nats** across the run — under CW the slot positions are enormously load-bearing.
Yet zeroing the plan content costs 0.0053 and shuffling which plan sits at which
slot costs 0.0001. The coda built 0.8 nats of machinery ON the slot positions
(registers / anchors / E_slot-conditioned compute) while reading nothing FROM
them. This is the CW spec's CW2 lesson ("the slot is merely a surviving
position") reproduced inside a training run, with the strongest planner we have
ever attached.

## Verdict

The pre-registered decisive negative fired: worth ≈ 0 with the cheap channel
deleted. The planner is exonerated twice over (FM1: accurate but unread with
tokens present; FM1-CW: accurate but unread with tokens absent). The fault is
in the write/read interface, not the writer:

- The plan reaches the coda ONLY as `W_prefix(z)` added at prefix positions,
  and `z` is detached — nothing ever aligns the planner's output basis with a
  basis the coda's attention can decode. CE trains `W_prefix` and the coda, but
  they evidently minimize loss by using the positions as registers and treating
  the z-dependent component as noise.
- Honest counterpoint recorded: worth_zero rose slowly and roughly linearly late
  in the run (~0.001 per 500 steps). At that slope the gate is ~10k+ steps away.
  An observation, not a promise; the register effect (0.81 nats) grew 100×
  faster over the same steps.

## Updated hypothesis + the named next step

The next experiment is the ORACLE PREFIX PROBE the prereg named: plant a vector
that provably contains the dropped content (the bag-mean of a dropped span's
token embeddings — the exact statistic the slot state approximated in the
2026-08-18 measurements) at the slot prefixes of a CW model, and measure CE.
- Oracle pays ⇒ the pathway can decode content; the fault is the detached
  planner basis, and the fix is a trainable alignment (e.g. train W_prefix
  against the oracle target, or un-detach a LOW-BANDWIDTH projection of z).
- Oracle does not pay ⇒ the additive-prefix interface itself cannot deliver
  content to the coda, and the fix is architectural (plans as first-class
  tokens or a cross-attention read path), before any further planner work.
