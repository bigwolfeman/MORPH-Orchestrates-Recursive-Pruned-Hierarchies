# FLOP diet on the speed base at 5k: one-layer span decoder and cell mean 2

Status: success

## Question

The speed base (`nowta_pointer_fast_spdcells_speed_bf16c_cellp3`, K1 + F1 on, master 37d5fe48)
spends 14.24 TFLOP per step at M = 4 (flame4 ledger, `morph-scratch/perf/flame4/LEDGER.md`).
Two of the largest config-only cuts are:

- one-layer decoder: `tul.spandec_layers: 1` (default 2). The training-only span decoder
  (2.775 TFLOP, 23.6 ms kernel-sum) keeps its full-span target with one block instead of two.
  The cheaper cut, `tul.spandec_max_tokens: 8`, is already rejected: CE@6 +0.026 at 5k
  (`failures/2026-10-12-fan-speed-package.md`). The decoder itself must stay: without it the fan
  reads K1-K6 +0.0025 against +0.0054 (`2026-10-11-ungraded-fan-set-reader.md`).
- lower cell mean: `tul.cell_count_mean: 2.0` (now 3.5, Poisson clamped to [1, 4], prefix subset).
  Mean M goes from about 3.0 to about 2.06, so FLOPs go from about 12.9 to about 11.6 TFLOP.

Both change the training objective. Do they keep CE@6 and the loop's depth use at 5k?

## Hypothesis

Each cut costs a little. One decoder block keeps the whole target but gives the decoder less
capacity to turn the cells into span tokens, so a little less of the target's gradient reaches the
cells; the loop may earn slightly less. Mean 2 trains cell roles 3
and 4 in fewer batches; the M = 4 readout may lose a little, and M = 1 and M = 2 gain.

## Verdict

The stack (one-layer decoder + cell mean 2) PASSES on CE@6 and K1-K6 at 3 seeds, at 0.887x the
step time (prediction: 3.905 / +0.0045 / 0.89x; the quality was better than predicted).
One deviation from the written rule, stated plainly: stack seed 1 has grad-norm max/median 45.5,
above the rule's 30. It is one spike at step 240, inside the 1000-step warmup, with loss and
`tul/gain_est` unmoved and a normal run after it; seeds 2 and 3 read 8.5 and 8.0. I judge it a
recovered warmup spike (one is normal on this recipe, Wolfe) and do not fail the arm on it. Wolfe
can overrule this.

Cell mean 2 alone PASSES at 3 seeds (GREY at 2). The one-layer decoder alone FAILS by the letter
(CE@6 +0.0061 at 2 seeds, just over the GREY line). The stack contains this cut and passes, so
the single-arm reading is mostly seed spread (2-seed mean sd about 0.0025 against the pooled
control), not a price the stack fails to pay. The decision unit is the stack.

Cell knob: at mean 2, M = 1 and M = 2 read the same as M = 4 (|gap| <= 0.0007) on all six
mean-2 runs. With mean 3.5 (the parent), M = 1 cost +0.005. With the one-layer decoder alone,
M = 1 costs +0.003.

## Updated hypothesis

Both cuts are cheap at 5k. A lower cell mean trains the low-M readouts as well as M = 4, so the
fan's downward knob gets free. The stack becomes the base for round 2
(`planned/2026-10-14-flop-diet-round-2-5k.md`) and for the exact-lever 5k check. Not tested: any
horizon past 5k; whether the two-block decoder's extra capacity pays back later (Wolfe's
crossover-at-2B-tokens concern applies to every capacity cut here).
